1. Who pays for the remaining work?
  Establishes the book’s central argument: successful delegation doesn’t merely move effort onto someone else. Maintainer economics, accountability, handoff, and continuity all depend on
  this.

2. What makes software high-quality?
  Defines what you’re trying to preserve—not just functional correctness, but comprehensibility, maintainability, and fitness for purpose. Specifications, verification, and governance
  need this shared standard.

3. Building a theory
  Explains what understanding the human needs to retain. This gives substance to “LLMs are not a substitute for thought,” human authority, and avoiding wild goose chases.

4. Specifications as design work → Verification as feedback loop
  Draft these together: what outcome do you intend, and what evidence would establish it? They supply the practical mechanism that process engineering, continual improvement, and
  mechanical governance can build on.

5. Maintainer economics → The reviewable patch
  Test the preceding ideas against another person’s limited attention. A concrete contribution example will expose whether your responsibility argument produces useful practices or
  merely exhortations.

6. Human authority
  Establish who decides, approves, and remains able to intervene. Use that to organize sandboxing, change governance, and operational continuity.
