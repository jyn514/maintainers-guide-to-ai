# Who pays for the remaining work?

- Generating an artifact is not the same as completing the work: someone must establish whether it solves the problem, integrate it, and live with it.
- Count investigation, review, support, and maintenance alongside production time. A faster draft can still make the whole task more expensive.
- Distinguish agreed delegation from silently transferring unresolved work. Collaboration need not mean doing everything yourself.
- Example to develop: an agent produces a plausible bug fix without a reproduction. The reviewer must discover whether the bug exists, whether the patch fixes it, and what else it changes.
  - Contrast with a contributor who supplies a reproduction, a bounded fix, and checked results; the reviewer can spend attention on the actual decision.
- Practice: before handing off, name what remains uncertain, who will resolve it, and whether they have agreed to take it on. Stop or narrow the work if you cannot support it.
- You retain responsibility for the claims you make about generated work; disclosure does not establish those claims.
